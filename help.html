RLBPred-Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Welcome to the RLBPDB Help section. Below, you’ll find answers to common questions about using the website and its features.

What are R-loops and why are they important?
R-loops are three-stranded nucleic acid structures consisting of an RNA: DNA hybrid and a displaced single-stranded DNA (ssDNA). They are essential in various biological processes but can also pose a risk to genomic stability, leading to neurodegenerative disorders and cancers if not properly regulated.

How does the RLBPPred model relate to this website?
RLBPPred is a machine learning model we’ve developed to predict the binding affinity of R-loop Binding Proteins (RLBPs) to R-loops. It is trained on a selective dataset and provides probability scores for R-loop binding ability. On this website, you can search for the binding probabilities calculated by the model for 20,076 human proteins. Each protein entry includes detailed information such as UniProtKB ID, UniProtKB Accession, Protein Name, RefSeq, GenPept, Source Organism, Gene Name, Map Location, Proteomes, and amino acid sequences.
You can explore the prediction results directly through the database or download the dataset for further analysis.

How can I search for a specific protein?
You can search for proteins using the Quick Search or Search features:
• In Home page, you can slide down to Quick Search and input a Protein ID, Gene Name, or Protein Sequence for fast and direct results.
• On the Search Page, choose from multiple keyword options like Protein ID (UniProtKB ID, Protein Name, RefSeq, etc.) or Gene Name, or use the sequence-based fuzzy search to match protein sequences. If you cannot find your protein of interest in the database, you can visit www.chhhhh.com to use our model-RLBPred and calculate the probability of the protein binding to R-loop structures.

How does the Protein Sequence search work?
When searching by Protein Sequence, the system performs a fuzzy match, comparing your input with other sequences in the database. The sequence with the highest similarity to your query will be highlighted, making it easier for you to compare sequences and identify the protein of interest. Results are returned based on match accuracy, and you can browse through a table of proteins, viewing details as needed.

What can I download from the Download page?
You can download protein data and predictions in both JSON and Excel formats. The files contain information such as:
• UniProtKB ID
• UniProtKB Accession
• Protein Name
• RefSeq
• GenPept
• Source Organism
• Gene Name
• Map Location
• Proteomes
• Amino acid sequences
• Predicted binding affinity to R-loops
